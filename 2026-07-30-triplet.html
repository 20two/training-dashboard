Analysis

The verdict

Strong session — 14:54, dead center of your 14–17 target, HR controlled the whole way, no blow-up. As a controlled aerobic piece the day after a rest day, this did its job. But the pacing target you missed is the interesting part, because it's the exact mirror of McGhee: there you went out too slow and finished with a gear left; here you went out too fast and faded. Two workouts, two opposite errors, one root cause — your pace-by-feel instinct misleads you at the start of a piece.

The bike told on you

I said the bike was the dial and to keep it repeatable. Your bike splits: 2:25.6 → 2:56.8 → 3:06.5. That's a 41-second positive split — you opened ~30% hotter than you could hold and bled it back over the next two rounds. That round-1 bike (2:26) is the "devil himself is chasing you" pace Pat warned about. It felt fast and free in the moment; it wasn't.

The bike was the wall — confirmed in your words

Your own account nails it: you came out hot on the round-1 bike and slowly bled the pace, then rounds 2–3 you "hit the wall" with major quad burn — backing off, catching yourself, surging in bursts, backing off again. That ragged surge-and-fade is the signature of a too-fast open: once the quads went, you couldn't hold a line, so you paid in stop-start pacing. The bike splits confirm it — 2:26 → 2:57 → 3:07 — and the fade was local (quads), not cardiac: your HR stayed controlled the whole time. Same theme as McGhee's push-ups — your engine is fine, but when you over-pace early, the specific driver muscle (chest there, quads here) is what walls out.

One correction from your notes, and a good data catch: that 80-second round-2 jump rope wasn't a performance crater — it's a Tempus transition artifact. The bike hits 25 cal, Tempus instantly logs it and advances to the next round's rope before you've walked over, so the walk-over got counted into the rope split. Your rope was actually near-flawless all day: deliberate 25/25/25 sets by choice, and in round 3 you went for unbroken and only tripped at rep 53. Rope and sit-ups went exactly to plan. Worth remembering for every future read: the first movement of each round after the bike will read slow because it's eating the transition — I'll discount that going forward.

What went right

Your engine was controlled and honest. Max HR 162 (vs 171 in McGhee), average 148, and it climbed cleanly 143 → 152 → 156 across rounds without spiking into a forced stop. This wasn't a cardio blow-up — you had the fitness. The gap was purely distribution. And the movement choices held up: AbMat sit-ups stayed steady (1:02 / 1:05 / 1:11 — minimal fade), and the bike, your best-grooved movement, carried 57% of the work exactly as expected.

The fix (now doubly confirmed)

Same lesson as McGhee, and now you've proven it twice: pick your pace before rep 1 and hold it — especially on the monostructural piece. For this workout, the right open was a bike around 2:50, not 2:25. If you can honestly picture doing a 4th round at your round-1 pace, you paced it right; if round 1 feels notably easier than you expected the finish to, you're going out too hot. Even pacing here likely would have killed the round-2 crater and put you around 14:30 without feeling worse — but more importantly, it builds the pacing discipline that's now your clearest recurring gap.

The trend worth naming

Across two sessions, your fitness isn't the limiter — your pacing discipline is. McGhee: too slow early. Today: too fast early. That's now a KPI worth tracking on its own. Next controlled piece, the win condition isn't the clock — it's three splits within ~15s of each other. Hit that and the clock takes care of itself.
